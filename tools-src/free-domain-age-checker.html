What is a domain age checker?

A domain age checker tells you how long ago a domain name was first registered. It works as a website age checker too: type in any site and you can see how old its domain is, when the registration runs out and which registrar holds it. Our tool reads the public registration record through RDAP, the modern replacement for the old WHOIS lookup, and adds the date the Internet Archive first saved the site.

How to check domain age

  1. Type or paste the domain, for example example.com. A full web address works too, and we strip the rest.
  2. Press Check age.
  3. Read the age, registration date, expiry date and registrar in the result card.
  4. Compare the registration date with the first archived date to see roughly when the site went live.

What the results mean

  • Registered: the domain creation date, the day the domain was first registered. Domain age is counted from here.
  • Expires: the domain registration date runs out then. Domains that are not renewed can be lost.
  • Last updated: when the registration record last changed, for example after a renewal or a change of name servers.
  • Registrar: the company the domain is registered through.
  • First archived: the earliest snapshot of the site in the Wayback Machine, a useful clue to domain history.
  • Status and nameservers: transfer locks and the servers the domain points to.

Domain age vs website age

These are not always the same. A domain can be registered years before a website is built on it, or it can expire and be picked up by a new owner. To work out how old a website really is, look at both the registration date and the first archived date. If the archive shows content long after the registration date, the site is younger than its domain.

Why check a website's age?

  • Buying an aged domain: confirm the age and look at the history before you pay a premium for it.
  • Vetting a supplier, shop or competitor: a business that has been online for a decade is a different proposition from one that appeared last month.
  • Spotting suspicious sites: many scam sites use recently registered domains. A brand-new domain is a reason to be careful, not proof of fraud.
  • SEO audits: know the history of a domain before a redesign or a migration.
  • Protecting your own domain: check the expiry date so you never lose it by accident.

Does domain age affect SEO?

Google has said that age alone is not a ranking factor. Older domains often do better because they have had more time to collect links, content and trust, not because of the date itself.
