Website for Restaurant in Kolkata: Cost and Features

12 November 2026 · 9 min read

Planning a website for your restaurant in Kolkata? Costs in rupees, must-have features, Zomato and Swiggy fee savings, and a launch checklist.

A website for a restaurant in Kolkata should show your menu with prices, your address, opening hours, a map, real photos and one clear way to order or book. A simple menu page costs from about ₹8,000. A full restaurant website starts around ₹20,000, and one with direct online ordering starts around ₹50,000.

The ordering version can pay for itself. Zomato and Swiggy commissions are reported at roughly 16% to 30% of each order, so every order that moves to your own site keeps most of that money. This guide covers what to include, what it costs, how the fee savings work, and how to get found when someone searches for restaurants near them in Kolkata.

What should a restaurant website in Kolkata include?

Many diners look a restaurant up on their phone before they visit. They want the same few answers fast.

  1. A menu written as text on the page, with prices. A PDF is slow on mobile and Google reads it poorly.
  2. Your address, opening hours and a map link. Name the neighbourhood, such as Park Street, Gariahat, Salt Lake or New Town, so people searching by area can find you.
  3. A tap-to-call button and a WhatsApp button at the top of every page.
  4. Real photos of your food and your dining room. Stock photos make a restaurant look like every other one.
  5. A way to book a table or place an order, depending on how you earn.
  6. Your cuisine, price range and any dietary options, such as vegetarian or Jain, stated plainly.
  7. Customer reviews, with a link to your Google listing.
  8. Your FSSAI licence number in the footer. It works as a trust signal.

If your customers read Bengali, offer the menu in both Bengali and English. It costs little and makes the site easier to use for your regulars.

How much does a restaurant website cost in Kolkata?

Published prices vary widely. Indian agencies list anything from ₹15,000 for a basic site to more than ₹4 lakh for a custom build with ordering, reservations and chatbots. Most single-outlet restaurants do not need the top end.

It depends on where your sales come from today.

How much can you save compared with Zomato and Swiggy?

Reports put Zomato and Swiggy commissions at roughly 16% to 30% of the order value. A Bengaluru restaurant association has cited a range of 8% to 28%, plus advertising and payment fees. Your own rate depends on your contract, so check your latest statement.

Orders placed on your own site avoid that commission. You still pay the payment gateway, which commonly takes around 2% of each online payment. Here is a worked example with assumed numbers.

With 600 delivery orders a month at ₹450 each, sales are ₹2,70,000. At 20% commission you pay ₹54,000. If 120 of those orders come through your own site, you avoid ₹10,800 in commission and pay about ₹1,080 in gateway fees, a net saving of roughly ₹9,720 a month. A ₹50,000 ordering site would pay back in about five months under these assumptions. These numbers are an example, not a promise. Your order volume and the share of customers who switch will decide the real figure.

Do not delist from the apps on day one. They bring you new customers who have never heard of you. The usual approach is to stay listed, then give regulars a reason to order direct, such as a small discount, a free item, or a QR code on the packaging that leads to your ordering page.

How does a restaurant website get found in Kolkata?

A new website will not outrank Zomato for "best restaurants in Kolkata". It can rank for your own name, your neighbourhood and your specific dishes, and these steps help most:

  1. Claim and complete your Google Business Profile. For "restaurants near me" searches, it matters more than your website. Add photos, hours, your menu link and your phone number.
  2. Keep your name, address and phone number identical on your website, Google, Zomato and Swiggy.
  3. Put the menu on the page as text, and name your dishes the way customers search for them, such as "kosha mangsho" or "chingri malai curry".
  4. Ask your developer to add Restaurant structured data with your cuisine, opening hours and address. It helps Google and AI answers read your details correctly.
  5. Make the site fast on mobile data. Compress photos and avoid heavy sliders.
  6. Ask happy customers for Google reviews, and reply to every review.

AI tools such as ChatGPT and Google's AI answers pull from pages that state facts clearly, and from reviews and listings on other sites. A complete Google profile and consistent details across the web help there too. No one can promise a position, and results take weeks or months.

What do Kolkata restaurants often get wrong?

  • Special hours are not updated for festival days. Opening hours often change around Durga Puja and other festivals, and a wrong closing time sends diners elsewhere.
  • The menu is a photo of a printed card, so it cannot be searched or read easily on a phone.
  • There is no click-to-call button, so customers have to copy the number.
  • The ordering page sends the customer to a third-party app instead of taking the order itself.

Restaurant website launch checklist

  1. Menu with current prices, live on the page as text.
  2. Address, hours, festival hours and map link are correct.
  3. Call and WhatsApp buttons work on a real phone.
  4. A test order and a test payment go through, and you receive the alert.
  5. Google Business Profile links to the site.
  6. FSSAI number, privacy notice and refund or cancellation terms are in the footer.
  7. Pages load in a few seconds on mobile data.
  8. You can update the menu yourself, or you know who does it and how long it takes.

How long does it take to build a restaurant website?

A one-page menu site takes about 3 to 7 days, a full restaurant website 2 to 3 weeks, and a site with ordering and payments 4 to 6 weeks. Delays usually come from waiting for photos, menu text and approvals.

Not always. A dine-in restaurant often does well with a menu, photos, map and a booking or WhatsApp button. Online ordering makes sense if you sell a lot through delivery apps and want to reduce commission, or if you run a cloud kitchen.
