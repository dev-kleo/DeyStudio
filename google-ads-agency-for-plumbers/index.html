Google Ads Agency for Plumbers: How to Choose

What a Google Ads agency for plumbers should do, what a plumbing lead costs in 2026, how agencies charge, and the questions to ask before you sign.

A good Google Ads agency for plumbers does four things. It builds separate campaigns for emergency calls and planned jobs, it tracks phone calls and booked jobs instead of clicks, it keeps the ad account in your name, and it charges a fee you can predict. An agency that skips any of these will spend your budget on clicks that never ring your phone.

This guide covers what an agency actually does for a plumbing business, what a lead costs according to published 2026 data, how agencies charge, ten questions to ask before you sign, and the red flags that should end the conversation. Dollar figures come from US data. Costs in the UK, Canada and India differ, so read the numbers as ranges for comparison.

What a Google Ads agency does for a plumbing business

Most of the work happens before the first click. A plumbing account needs a structure that matches how customers search and how you dispatch jobs.

  • Separate search campaigns for emergency work (burst pipe, blocked drain, no hot water) and planned work (water heater replacement, bathroom fit-out, repiping). Emergency searchers tend to call whoever answers first. Planned-job searchers have more time to compare prices and reviews.
  • Service-area targeting that follows the postcodes or zip codes where you actually earn money.
  • Negative keywords that stop your ads showing for searches like "plumber jobs", "plumbing course" or "how to fix a dripping tap".
  • Call tracking and conversion tracking, so every call and form submission is recorded against the ad that produced it.
  • One landing page per main service, each with a phone number and a form above the fold.
  • Local Services Ads set-up and upkeep, in countries where Google offers them.
  • A report that shows calls, booked jobs and cost per booked job, not only clicks and impressions.

The agency does not answer your phone. Whether a lead turns into a job depends on your response time, and we come back to that below.

Search ads or Local Services Ads for plumbers

Search ads appear when someone types a query such as "emergency plumber near me". You bid on keywords and pay for each click. Local Services Ads sit above the standard results in some countries. They show your business name, reviews and a Google Guaranteed badge, and you pay for each lead.

Before you plan around Local Services Ads, check that Google offers them in your country, because the program is not available everywhere. Where it is, response speed and reviews affect how often you appear, so answering quickly helps the ad as well as the lead.

What a plumbing lead costs in 2026

Two numbers get confused. Cost per click is what Google charges when someone taps your ad. Cost per lead is what you pay for each call or form that arrives, and it is always higher because only a share of clicks turn into a lead.

The spread between $20 and $256 is wide. The sources probably count leads differently and draw on different markets and time periods, so do not hold an agency to any single figure. Ask them for the range in your own city and for the job type you want most.

The cheapest lead is not the best one. The Ad Spend, summarising the same SearchLight data, points out that at an 18.4% paying rate a $107 lead that never books is worth less than a $250 lead that does. Measure cost per booked job.

You can work out what you can afford to pay with a small calculation. Take the profit on the average job, multiply it by the share of leads that become jobs, and you have your maximum cost per lead. For example, if a typical job earns $400 in profit and one lead in three books, your ceiling is about $133 per lead. If your landing page turns 10% of clicks into leads and a click costs $10, each lead costs about $100, which fits. At a 5% conversion rate the same click costs $200 per lead, which does not. That is why landing pages come up in every serious conversation about plumbing ads.

How Google Ads agencies charge plumbers

Agency fees are separate from your ad spend. Google bills the spend to your ad account, and the agency bills you for management. Agencies usually choose one of three models: a flat monthly fee, a percentage of ad spend (commonly 10% to 20%), or a hybrid of the two.

A flat fee is easy to budget for. Ask what it includes, because some flat fees cover the campaigns only and charge extra for landing pages and call tracking. A percentage fee grows with your budget, which means the agency earns more whenever clicks get more expensive. The PPC agency ppc.io makes this point about the plumbing market, where emergency clicks are contested. Neither model is wrong, but you should know which one you are signing.

Published budget advice varies. Clicks Geek says established plumbers typically spend $4,000 to $8,000 a month, while Housecall Pro suggests starting with $500 to $1,000 a month for a small operation. Neither gives a source for the figure, so work from your own numbers: the cost-per-lead ceiling above, and how many jobs you can take each week.

Ten questions to ask before you hire a Google Ads agency

  1. Will the Google Ads account be in my name, and will I have admin access from day one?
  2. What is the fee, what does it cover, and what costs extra?
  3. How long is the contract, and how much notice do I need to leave?
  4. Do you run separate campaigns for emergency and planned work?
  5. How do you track calls, and do you record which calls became booked jobs?
  6. Do you set up and manage Local Services Ads where they are available?
  7. How often do you review the search terms report and add negative keywords?
  8. Will you build a landing page for each main service, or send everyone to my homepage?
  9. What will your report show each month, and can I see the raw account at any time?
  10. How do you handle calls that come in after hours?

An honest agency answers all ten in writing without hesitation. If the answers are vague, that tells you how the reporting will look.

Red flags when choosing a plumbing PPC agency

Some warning signs show up in the first call.

  • A guarantee of first place on Google or a fixed number of leads. Nobody controls auctions or your competitors, so no agency can promise either.
  • A refusal to give you login access, or an ad account registered in the agency's name. If you leave, you lose your campaign history and data.
  • A twelve-month contract with no exit. Month-to-month terms force an agency to keep earning the work.
  • Reports that list clicks, impressions and click-through rate but not calls or booked jobs.
  • One campaign for every service. Faucet repairs and sewer replacements have very different values, and a single budget treats them as equal.
  • A pitch that mixes ads with shared lead lists. Lead aggregators such as Angi and HomeAdvisor sell the same lead to several plumbers, so your close rate falls and your real cost rises.

What to fix before you pay for clicks

Ads send people to a phone and a page. If either is weak, more spend does not help.

Answer the phone. A plumber who misses calls pays for the click and loses the job to the next listing. If nobody can pick up, a voicemail that promises a call back within the hour is better than silence.

Give each service its own page. A visitor who searches "water heater replacement" should land on a page about water heater replacement, with your phone number, your service area and your prices or price range. Collect reviews and complete your Google Business Profile. Local Services Ads display your reviews, and searchers read them in the standard results as well.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a Google Ads agency for plumbers cost?

Management fees are separate from ad spend. Agencies charge a flat monthly fee, a percentage of ad spend (commonly 10% to 20%), or a mix of both. Ask for the fee and what it covers in writing, and compare the total with the cost per lead you can afford.

How much should a plumber spend on Google Ads each month?

There is no standard amount. Published guides range from $500 to $1,000 a month for a small operation to $4,000 to $8,000 for established firms. Work out your maximum cost per lead from your average job profit and the share of leads that book, then divide your target number of leads into that.

Are Local Services Ads better than Google Ads for plumbers?

They work differently. Local Services Ads charge per lead, show your reviews and a Google Guaranteed badge, and require a background and licence check. Search ads give you more control over keywords, wording and landing pages. Most plumbing agencies run both where Local Services Ads are available.

How long does it take to get calls from Google Ads?

Search ads can start sending visitors within days of launch. Judge the results only after a few weeks of data, and give a campaign at least 30 days before you change the plan. Calls depend on your market, your offer, your page and how fast you answer.

Can I run Google Ads for my plumbing business myself?

Yes, and many plumbers do. The risk is waste, because a missed negative keyword or an unfocused campaign spends money quickly. If you try it yourself, set up call tracking first and read the search terms report every week.

Should I use lead services like Angi instead of Google Ads?

Lead services sell the same lead to several plumbers, so you compete for each one and your effective cost rises. Google Ads leads come straight from searches and are not resold to several plumbers, but you carry the work of tracking and improving the account. Many plumbers use both and compare cost per booked job.
