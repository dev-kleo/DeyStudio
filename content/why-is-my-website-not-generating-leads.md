If you are asking why your website is not generating leads, the cause is almost always one of three things. The leads are being lost before they reach you, the wrong people are visiting, or the page gives the right people no reason to act. Each one can be found in about 30 minutes and fixed without a rebuild, in most cases.

This guide goes in the order we use on client sites. We start with the checks that cost nothing, move to the nine most common causes, and finish with a step-by-step diagnosis you can run today. Our shorter piece, [why your business website gets visitors but no customers](/why-your-website-gets-no-customers/), covers the five fastest fixes if you only have ten minutes.

## Check you are not losing leads you already get

Before you change any copy or design, make sure the website can actually deliver a lead. A surprising number of "no leads" cases are a form that never sends or an email that lands in spam.

Do this on your phone, not your laptop, because most visitors will be on a phone:

- Fill in your own contact form and submit it. Wait ten minutes. Check the inbox and the spam folder of the address that should receive it.
- Tap your phone number. It should open the dialer.
- Tap your WhatsApp button. It should open a chat with the right number.
- Open the site on mobile data, not your home wi-fi, and note how long the first screen takes to appear.

Then open Google Analytics 4 and look at the key events report. If form submits, calls and WhatsApp taps are not marked as key events, you cannot tell a conversion problem from a measurement problem. Set them up first. Otherwise every number you read after this is a guess.

## Which kind of problem do you have?

The symptom tells you where to look. Match what you see in your analytics to the row below, then jump to the numbered reason.

| What you see | Likely problem | Start with |
|---|---|---|
| Very few visitors | People cannot find you | Reason 1 |
| Plenty of visitors, almost nobody clicks contact | The offer, the next step or trust | Reasons 3, 4 and 7 |
| People click contact but few forms arrive | Form, speed or tracking | Reasons 5 and 6 |
| Enquiries arrive but none turn into customers | Wrong traffic or slow follow-up | Reasons 2 and 9 |
| Ad clicks but no enquiries | Page does not match the ad | Reason 8 |

## 9 reasons your website is not generating leads

### 1. Too few people who want to buy can find you

A website needs visitors before it can get leads, and visitors who already know your name are rarely enough. If most of your traffic is people typing your business name, nobody new is finding you.

To check, open the Search Console performance report and look at the queries that bring clicks. If almost all of them include your brand name, you have a visibility problem. The fix is a page for each service and each place you serve, written around the phrase a customer would type, such as "website design cost in Kolkata" instead of "our solutions". Paid search can fill the gap while that grows. Our [Google Ads management](/google-ads-management/) page explains how we run it for small budgets.

### 2. The traffic is the wrong traffic

Ten thousand visitors who wanted a free template or a job at a web company will never become leads. Traffic from the wrong search terms also drags your conversion rate down, which makes a healthy page look broken.

Check the landing pages report in GA4 and the queries in Search Console. Ask of each top query: would this person pay us? If a blog post about a free tool gets most of your visits, that is not a failure, but it will not produce enquiries unless the post leads somewhere. Either point it at a relevant service page or stop counting it against your lead rate. In ads, add negative keywords for words like "free", "jobs", "course" and "salary".

### 3. Visitors cannot tell what you do in five seconds

Visitors decide quickly whether a page is for them. If your top banner says "Innovative solutions for a digital world", they will not stay to find out.

The first screen needs four things: what you do, who it is for, where, and what to do next. "Websites and Google Ads for restaurants in Kolkata. Message us for a free review" says all four. Show the first screen to someone outside your business for five seconds, then ask what you sell. If they cannot answer, rewrite the headline before touching anything else.

### 4. There is no obvious next step

A visitor who is interested will not hunt for a way to contact you. A contact link buried in the footer, three different buttons with three different labels, or a button that scrolls to nothing all lose enquiries.

Pick one main action per page and give it one label that says what happens next, such as "Get a free quote" or "Message us on WhatsApp". Put it in the header and after each major section. On mobile, keep it visible without scrolling far. Read your own labels critically: "Submit" and "Learn more" tell the visitor nothing.

### 5. The form is too long, or it is broken

Every extra field gives a visitor another reason to leave. A form that asks for a company size, a budget, a deadline and a phone number before you have said hello is a wall.

Start with name, one way to reach the person, and a free-text box for what they need. You can ask the rest on the first call. Then test it. Submit the form with a made-up entry on a phone, on a laptop, and with the browser's autofill. Check that the thank-you message appears, that the lead email arrives, and that it does not go to spam. If you collect personal data, add a short line saying how you will use it, which is also a legal requirement in many places.

### 6. The site is slow on a phone

A page that takes too long to show anything loses people before they read a word. Speed also feeds into how Google ranks the page, so slow pages lose twice.

Run your top landing page through PageSpeed Insights and read the mobile tab, not the desktop one. Google treats a largest contentful paint of 2.5 seconds or less as good. The usual causes are oversized images, too many scripts from plugins and chat widgets, and cheap shared hosting. Compress images, remove plugins you do not use, and load video only when someone presses play. Those three changes fix most slow sites without a redesign.

### 7. Nothing proves you are safe to hire

People who do not know you need a reason to trust you with money. A site with no names, no photos of real work and no reviews makes the visitor do all the risk assessment alone.

Add what you have, even if it is small: a client's name and a line about what changed, a screenshot of a Google review, the years you have worked, a real address and phone number, and the faces of the people who will do the work. If you are new and have none of this yet, offer a small first project at a clear price in exchange for a testimonial, and say so on the page. Invented testimonials do more harm than an empty section.

### 8. Every visitor lands on the homepage

If your ads, social posts and search listings all point to the homepage, each visitor has to work out where to go. A person who clicked an ad for "Google Ads for dentists" and lands on a general agency page will leave.

Send each source to the page that matches the promise. An ad about restaurant websites should land on a page about restaurant websites, with the same words in the headline and one clear action. The same applies to search: one page per service, each with its own title and enquiry button. Our [website design and development](/website-design-and-development/) page is an example of one service per page.

### 9. Leads arrive and go cold

Some websites are working and the leak comes after the form. If a lead waits a day for a reply, they have usually asked someone else by then. Missed calls and unread WhatsApp messages count too.

Set a rule you can keep. For example, reply to every enquiry within one working hour during business days. Turn on an instant auto-reply that confirms you got the message and says when to expect an answer. Keep a simple list of every enquiry with a status so nothing is forgotten. If you cannot reply fast, a short auto-reply that offers a booking link is better than silence.

## A 30-minute diagnosis, step by step

Work through these in order. Write down what you find; you will use the numbers later.

1. Open GA4 and go to Reports, Acquisition, Traffic acquisition. Set the date range to the last 90 days. Note which channels bring the visitors: search, social, direct, paid.
2. Note total sessions and total key events (form submits, calls, WhatsApp taps) for the same period. You need both for the next section.
3. Open the landing pages report. List your top five pages by sessions. Check that each one has a clear action above the fold.
4. On your phone, test every contact method, as described at the top of this guide.
5. Run the top landing page through PageSpeed Insights and save the mobile score and the largest contentful paint time.
6. Show the first screen of your homepage to one person outside your business for five seconds. Ask them what you sell and where.
7. Search for your main service and city on Google. Open the first three competitors and note how they present price, proof and the next step.

By the end you will have a list of specific faults. Fix the cheapest and most certain first: broken forms and tracking, then the headline and button, then speed, then proof, then new pages.

## How to work out your conversion rate

Conversion rate is the number of leads divided by the number of visitors, multiplied by 100. If 1,000 people visit in a month and 10 of them send an enquiry, your rate is 1%.

Count one lead per person, not per click, and decide in advance what a lead is. A form submit, a call from the site, and a WhatsApp message all qualify. A newsletter signup might not.

Published averages vary a lot by industry, traffic source and how each company defines a lead, so we do not give you a target number to chase. Compare against your own last 90 days instead. Make one change, wait for enough visitors to see a difference, and measure again. If your site gets only a few hundred visits a month, changes will take longer to show up, and you should judge them by the number of enquiries, not the percentage.

## Extra checks for local and India-based businesses

If your customers come from one city or region, three more things decide whether the phone rings.

First, your Google Business Profile. Fill in the categories, the service area, hours, photos and the website link, and reply to every review. For many local searches this listing is seen before your website is.

Second, WhatsApp. Offer a WhatsApp button next to the form, with a pre-filled message such as "Hi, I would like a quote for a website". Some visitors will never fill in a form but will send a message.

Third, location pages. A page titled "Web development company in Kolkata" with real details about the work you do there will match local searches better than a general services page. ## How long fixes take and when a redesign makes sense

Fixing a broken form or tracking takes hours and shows results the same week. Changes to the headline, the button and the form usually need a few weeks of traffic before you can judge them. New service and location pages that you hope will bring search traffic take months, because Google needs time to find and rank them. Ads can bring traffic quickly, but they need a matching landing page, or you pay for clicks that leave.

Most of the nine fixes are small jobs. A redesign is worth considering in a few cases: the site cannot be edited to change the headline or add pages, it does not work properly on phones, or it is built on a template that you have outgrown. Even then, the order above still applies, because a new design with the same unclear offer will get the same result. ## Frequently asked questions

### Why does my website get traffic but no leads?

The visitors are usually the wrong ones, or the page does not make the next step obvious. Check your top landing pages and the search terms bringing people in, then test your form and contact buttons on a phone. A broken form can look exactly like a site with no interest.

### What is a good conversion rate for a website?

It depends on your industry, where visitors come from, and what you count as a lead. A page with ready-to-buy search visitors will convert better than a blog post. Measure your own rate over the last 90 days and aim to beat that.

### How many leads should a website generate?

There is no fixed number. It depends on how many relevant people visit and how easy you make it to contact you. A better goal is that qualified enquiries rise month after month.

### Do I need a new website to get more leads?

Usually not. Most lead problems come from an unclear headline, a weak button, a long or broken form, slow mobile pages or missing proof. Fix these first and redesign only if the site cannot be changed to do so.

### How long does it take to see more leads after changes?

Fixes to forms and tracking show up within days. Copy and design changes take a few weeks of traffic to measure. Search-driven growth from new pages takes months.

### Should I buy leads while I fix my website?

You can run Google Ads to bring in enquiries while you fix the site, but send them to a page that matches the ad. Buying lists or shared leads rarely pays off, because the same lead is often sold to several businesses.
