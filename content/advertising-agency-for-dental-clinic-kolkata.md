Dentists in India cannot advertise the way a shop or a salon does. An advertising agency for a dental clinic in Kolkata has to work inside rules written by the dental regulator, and some of the things agencies commonly sell are the things those rules flag. The safe core is a factual website, accurate listings and tracking that shows which enquiries become appointments. The risky extras are patient testimonials, before-and-after photos, claims like "best dentist in Kolkata" and anything that reads as a promotion.

This guide explains the current rules, what an agency can reasonably do for a clinic, what dental leads cost, and how to choose an agency in Kolkata. It is not legal advice. The dentist is responsible for the clinic's publicity, so check any plan with your state dental council or a lawyer before it goes live.

## The rules that apply to dental advertising in 2026

The body that governs dentists changed this year. The National Dental Commission began operating on 19 March 2026. The Dentists Act, 1948 was repealed and the Dental Council of India was dissolved from the same date. The commission's Ethics and Dental Registration Board regulates professional conduct and registration of dentists.

The standard that board enforces on publicity is the Revised Dentists (Code of Ethics) Regulations, 2014. On 14 October 2026 the board issued an advisory to all registered dentists. It flagged recording or broadcasting consultations and procedures, publishing patient testimonials and before-and-after photographs, and making misleading treatment claims. It also named self-aggrandising publicity and disproportionately large signboards. The advisory cites Chapter VI of the 2014 regulations, clauses 8.1, 8.2 and 8.3, and says established violations may lead to disciplinary action, including suspension or removal from the State and National Register of Dentists.

The 2014 code is not a blanket ban. As we read it, it permits informational publicity and bars promotion.

| Generally allowed | Generally barred |
|---|---|
| A website with factual information about the clinic, treatments and fees | Claims that you are the "best" or "first" |
| Telephone directory and internet listings, as public information | Testimonials and before-and-after photographs |
| Displaying fees | Promising inducements, rebates or false benefits |
| Press announcements of a new practice, a new address or new equipment | Agents or canvassers who bring in patients for payment |
| Name, recognised qualifications and registration number | Using your own photograph in publicity |
| A "dental clinic" name, as the code prescribes | Calling yourself a specialist without a recognised qualification |

These points come from the code's clauses on advertising. The text on [websites and directory listings](https://tndentalcouncil.com/source/dentists_code_of_ethics_regulations_2014.pdf) says information must be factual and listings are public information only. Regulations move quickly after a change of regulator, so read the current text yourself before you rely on this table.

## What an advertising agency can do for a dental clinic within the rules

Most useful work is factual and measurable.

- Build a website that lists treatments, the dentists' registered qualifications and registration numbers, fees where you choose to show them, the clinic address and how to book. Keep the wording plain and avoid superlatives.
- Keep the Google Business Profile and other directory listings accurate and consistent, so a patient searching nearby finds the right address, phone number and hours.
- Write treatment pages that answer patient questions, such as what a root canal involves or when a crown is needed, in neutral language and without promises about outcomes.
- Set up call tracking, form tracking and WhatsApp tracking, so you know which enquiries come from where and how many become appointments.
- Report on cost per enquiry and cost per booked appointment, month by month.

Paid search is the area to discuss carefully. The parts of the code we read do not mention paid search ads by name. If you run Google Ads, keep the text factual: clinic name, treatment, location and phone number. Leave out offers, discounts, superlatives and patient quotes, and have the dentist approve every ad. ## What agencies sell that can put a dentist at risk

Some standard agency packages conflict with the advisory and the code. Ask about each one before you sign.

- Testimonial and review campaigns. The advisory lists publishing patient testimonials. Reposting patient reviews on your site or social pages is the part to ask your council about.
- Before-and-after galleries and procedure videos. Both are on the advisory's list.
- Social media reels that show treatments or consultations. The advisory flags recording and broadcasting them.
- "Best dentist in Kolkata" headlines and ad copy. The code bars claims of being the best or first.
- Influencer posts and paid promotion. The code bars demeaning solicitation, false promises and commercial canvassing, and agencies that charge per patient they deliver sit close to that line.
- Discount offers and "free check-up" banners. The code bars publicity that promises inducements, rebates or false benefits.
- Oversized signs and boards with extra wording. The advisory flags disproportionately large signboards.

An agency that promises quick results from any of these is selling you a risk to your registration.

The US numbers show the pattern. Searches for your own clinic name are cheap, general searches cost several times more, and high-value treatments such as implants cost the most per lead. Delmain reports that cost per lead swings about 28 times between treatment types. Indian prices are different, so ask any agency for a range in rupees for your area and for the treatments you want to promote.

The number to watch is the cost per new patient who books, not the cost per enquiry. Divide the cost per lead by the share of leads who become patients. For example, suppose a new patient brings ₹6,000 of treatment in the first year and one enquiry in four books. You can then pay up to ₹1,500 per enquiry before you lose money, and less once you count your margin.

## How to choose an advertising agency for a dental clinic in Kolkata

Treat the first meeting as a compliance check as well as a sales call.

1. Do you know the Revised Dentists (Code of Ethics) Regulations, 2014 and the National Dental Commission's October 2026 advisory? Ask them to explain both in plain words.
2. Will you publish testimonials, before-and-after photos or procedure videos for us? The right answer is no.
3. Who approves every ad, post and page before it goes live? It should be the dentist, in writing.
4. How do you charge, and is any part of your fee tied to the number of patients you deliver?
5. Will the Google Ads account, website and listings be in the clinic's name, with full admin access for us?
6. How do you track calls, forms and WhatsApp messages, and can we see which became appointments?
7. What does your monthly report show, and can we see the raw account at any time?
8. What is the contract length, and what notice do we need to give?
9. Have you built websites or listings for clinics in Kolkata, and can we see them?

An agency that answers all nine plainly and in writing has worked in regulated fields before. Vague answers on questions 1 to 3 are the biggest warning.

## A 90-day plan that stays inside the rules

This is our suggested order, not a regulatory requirement.

Month one is the foundation. Build or fix the website, correct the Google Business Profile and directory listings, and set up call, form and WhatsApp tracking. Our guide on [why a website is not generating leads](/why-is-my-website-not-generating-leads/) shows how to test the contact path on a phone.

Month two is content. Add one factual page for each main treatment and check each one against the code with the dentist. Look at the tracking to see which pages bring enquiries.

Month three is the decision on paid search. If you run it, start with a small budget on a few treatments, keep the ad text factual, and judge it by cost per booked appointment. If the data does not support the spend, stop and put the money into the website and listings.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can dental clinics advertise in India?

Only in a limited way. The Revised Dentists (Code of Ethics) Regulations, 2014 allow factual information such as a website, directory listings, fee display and announcements of a new practice or address. They bar boasting, "best" and "first" claims, testimonials, inducements and paid canvassing. The National Dental Commission restated this in an advisory on 14 October 2026.

### Who regulates dentists' advertising in 2026?

The National Dental Commission, through its Ethics and Dental Registration Board, took over from the Dental Council of India on 19 March 2026. State dental councils also act on complaints. In West Bengal that means the state dental council, so check its current notices.

### Are Google Ads allowed for a dental clinic in Kolkata?

The parts of the code we read do not mention paid search ads by name, so this is a question for your state dental council or a lawyer. If a clinic runs them, keep the text factual and avoid offers, superlatives and testimonials. The dentist should approve every ad.

### Can a dental clinic post before-and-after photos on Instagram?

The National Dental Commission's October 2026 advisory lists before-and-after photographs, patient testimonials and recorded procedures as conduct that can lead to disciplinary action. Educational, factual posts are a safer choice, and the dentist should check them against the code first.

### How much does dental marketing cost in Kolkata?

There is no reliable public price list for Kolkata. One Mumbai agency lists a comprehensive dental package at ₹35,000 to ₹65,000 a month. Management fees are separate from ad spend, so ask for the fee, what it covers and what costs extra, and compare the total with your cost per booked appointment.

### Can I pay an agency per patient it brings in?

We advise against it. The code bars using agents or canvassers to obtain patients commercially, and a per-patient fee looks very similar. A flat fee for defined work, such as a website, listings and tracking, is easier to defend.
